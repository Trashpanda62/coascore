Quick answers

Peptide COA questions, answered

Short answers, each with a longer guide behind it.

Research Use Only. This guide explains analytical documentation, not use in humans.

What does 99% purity on a peptide COA mean?

It is usually an HPLC area percentage: the target peak's area divided by the total area of all detected peaks under that method. It describes relative detector response. It is not the share of the vial's mass that is peptide, and it says nothing about water, salts or anything the detector does not see.

Can a COA show that a peptide is safe?

No. A COA documents what a laboratory measured on one submitted sample. CoaScore scores documentation quality only. It does not assess safety or suitability for use in humans, and everything here is for research use only.

What is net peptide content?

It is the mass of the peptide divided by the total mass of the powder, including water, counterions and residual salts. It is measured separately from purity, by a quantitative method such as amino acid analysis or quantitative NMR.

Is an HPLC result enough to trust a COA?

HPLC speaks to relative composition. A good report also supports identity, usually with mass spectrometry, and says how quantity was measured if a labeled amount is claimed. It should also name the method and tie every result to one sample and lot.

How do I check that a COA is genuine?

Match the lot to your vial, then confirm the report number with the issuing laboratory through its own website or verification channel, not through a link printed on the vendor's PDF. Also confirm the lab offers the assay named on the report.

What does "not detected" mean on a report?

It means the result was below the method's limit of detection. It does not prove the substance is absent. A value between the limit of detection and the limit of quantitation is detectable but cannot be reliably measured.

Why is a purity of exactly 100.0% a red flag?

Chromatographic results depend on detection, integration thresholds and rounding. A bare 100.0% with no full chromatogram, peak table or method detail gives you nothing to check, so it deserves a closer look.

What does the CoaScore 0-5 score measure?

How completely a COA documents identity, purity, amount accuracy, method detail and laboratory transparency. A higher score means more of the evidence is on the page. It is not a verdict on the product.

Where can I find the expected molecular weight of a peptide?

Each compound page in the peptide molecular weight reference lists the published molecular weight, formula and expected mass-spec peaks, with the source of the mass. For example, see semaglutide, tirzepatide, BPC-157 or TB-500. If you have only an observed mass or m/z from a COA, the reference's mass matcher suggests compounds that fit it.
