Peptide COA checklist

Tick each line while you read a certificate of analysis. A line you cannot tick is a question to ask the vendor or the lab, not proof of a problem. Full explanations: the reading guide.

Research Use Only. This checklist covers analytical documentation. It does not show a product is safe or suitable for use in humans.

1. Sample and lot

  • Product name, lot number and test date are on the report.
  • The lot matches the vial in front of you, exactly.
  • The issuing lab, report number and method names are stated.

2. Identity (mass spectrometry)

  • The report gives an observed mass or m/z and the expected value, plus the tolerance applied.
  • Average versus monoisotopic mass is stated, so like is compared with like. Check the expected value in the peptide molecular weight reference.
  • Sample ID and instrument are named on the spectrum.

3. Purity (HPLC)

  • A full-length chromatogram is shown, not a cropped window.
  • A peak table with retention times and areas adds up to the stated percentage.
  • Column, mobile phases, gradient, flow rate and wavelength are given.
  • The percentage is understood as area-%, not share of the vial's mass.

4. Quantity

  • If a vial amount is claimed, a separate content measurement exists (amino acid analysis, qNMR or a calibrated assay).
  • Its basis and salt form are stated. See net peptide content.
  • If there is no content line, the labeled amount is treated as the vendor's claim.

5. Authenticity

  • The report number was confirmed through the lab's own website or contact, not a link printed on the vendor's PDF.
  • The lab offers the assay named on the report.
  • Fonts, logos, dates and sample IDs are consistent across the document. More in COA red flags.
