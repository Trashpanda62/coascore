BPC-157 COA: molecular weight and what to check

Expected mass-spectrum peaks

A mass spectrometer reports m/z, not mass. The table applies (M + n × 1.00728) ÷ n to the reference mass above for each protonated charge state n. Peaks near these values support a mass of about 1,419.53 Da. Real spectra show some of these charge states, not all of them, and low charge states of larger molecules are often weak or absent. Sodium and potassium adducts and salt forms shift peaks, so a small offset is not automatically a mismatch; a large one is worth a question to the lab.

Cautions specific to this compound

  • Compound note. Synthetic pentadecapeptide (partial sequence of gastric protein BPC). Unmodified linear peptide — sequence-sum matches published MW. 'BPC-157 arginate' salt variants exist at different MW.
  • Salt and counter-ion. Commonly supplied as acetate salt; free-peptide MW listed. A vial's powder weight includes counter-ions and water, so the labeled amount is not necessarily the amount of peptide; see net peptide content.

What a COA for BPC-157 should show

  1. Identity. The report shows a mass-spectrum result and states an expected mass for BPC-157. Compare it with 1,419.53 Da (average) and the m/z table above.
  2. Purity. The report shows the HPLC trace, the method conditions and a peak table, not only a percentage. CoaScore's reference data lists a common purity claim of “≥ 98% HPLC”. A claim like that is relative composition, not vial quantity.
  3. Quantity. If the vial states an amount, the report says how it was measured. Purity and quantity are different numbers.
  4. Traceability. Lot number, sample ID, issuing lab, dates and report number match the vial and appear on every page of the PDF.
